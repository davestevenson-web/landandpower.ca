What we do

The groundwork behind every energy project.

Before construction begins, a project needs land secured, permits advanced, and power arranged. Land and Power carries out that early work. It is also the groundwork for national priorities like sovereign AI: the infrastructure Canadian companies need to compete globally, built here at home.

Site origination and land partnerships

Sites, Powered and Permitted

We screen land for energy projects, including generation, storage, and large-load developments, and work directly with landowners to bring the strongest sites to market. Landowner interests come first: a fair, transparent process that protects their interests and works to realize the full value of their land.

Permitting and approvals

We guide projects through municipal, provincial, and federal permitting and regulatory approvals, from early screening and pre-consultation through to final approval, including behind-the-meter generation projects.

Power and utility coordination

Power is the defining constraint on modern energy projects. We engage early with utilities and system operators on servicing, grid connection, and infrastructure coordination.

Independent diligence and advisory

For developers and investors evaluating energy project opportunities, we provide an independent read on permitting risk, power availability, and development timelines. This is the same screening discipline we apply to our own pipeline, offered as a service.

Method

How we screen.

Every site we consider goes through the same screening process before any decision is made. The process is gated, which means a site has to meet the standard at each stage before it can move to the next. We use our own screening tools and analytical methods, applied the same way every time, so that results can be compared and decisions can be explained.

The purpose is to make sure the sites that go forward are strong sites, and a good fit for the communities they would join. Most sites do not get through the process, and that is what the process is for.

The new rules

Projects now start inside a new rulebook.

Ontario has set clear expectations for large new loads: municipal support, full cost recovery for connection infrastructure, and a credible community-benefit case. Federal principles add environmental and community expectations of their own. Some municipalities have paused new applications while they write local rules. Projects that start inside the rules move. Projects that discover the rules late stop.

Approach

Bringing land, permits and power together.

Every energy project needs three things: the right land, the right permits, and the right power. We bring all three together before a project ever breaks ground.

01 Identify

Screen sites for size, location, servicing, permitting risk, and development potential, and build genuine partnerships with the landowners behind them.

02 De-risk

Advance permitting, regulatory approvals, and utility engagement early, when issues are cheapest to solve.

03 Deliver

Hand over a site that is ready to build on, with the land, approvals, and power picture a developer needs to move forward with certainty. Our role ends at that point.

Why now

Three shifts are making the early work the decisive work.

Electrification

Electricity demand is growing again after decades of flat load. Every new large load competes for the same grid capacity, and arranging a connection takes time.

AI and data centers

AI is driving a new class of large, always-on electricity loads. These projects need sites with power certainty on timelines the grid was not originally planned for.

Storage and onsite generation

Batteries and behind-the-meter generation are becoming part of how large loads get connected, not just how the grid stays balanced. Projects that plan for them early keep more options open.
