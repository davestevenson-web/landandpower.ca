What we do

The groundwork behind every energy project.

Before construction begins, a project needs land secured, permits advanced, and power arranged. Land and Power carries out that early work. It is also the groundwork for national priorities like sovereign AI: the infrastructure Canadian companies need to compete globally, built here at home.

Site origination and land partnerships

Sites, Powered and Permitted

We screen land for energy projects, including generation, storage, and large-load developments, and work directly with landowners to bring the strongest sites to market. Landowner interests come first: a fair, transparent process that protects their interests and works to realize the full value of their land.

Permitting and approvals

We guide projects through municipal, provincial, and federal permitting and regulatory approvals, from early screening and pre-consultation through to final approval, including behind-the-meter generation projects.

Power and utility coordination

Power is the defining constraint on modern energy projects. We engage early with utilities and system operators on servicing, grid connection, and infrastructure coordination.

Independent diligence and advisory

For developers and investors evaluating energy project opportunities, we provide an independent read on permitting risk, power availability, and development timelines. This is the same screening discipline we apply to our own pipeline, offered as a service.

Method

How we screen.

Every site we consider is screened before any decision is made, using our own tools and methods, applied consistently so results can be compared and decisions can be explained. Most sites do not make it through. The ones that do are well suited to development, and a good fit for the communities they would join.

The new rules

Projects now start inside a new rulebook.

Ontario's Data Centre Playbook sets clear expectations for large new loads: municipal support, full cost recovery for connection infrastructure, and a credible community-benefit case. The federal Responsible Data Centre Development Principles add environmental and community expectations of their own. Some municipalities have paused new applications while they develop local rules. In practice, projects planned within these rules from the outset proceed, while projects that encounter them late tend to stall.

Why now

Three shifts are reshaping energy project development.

Electrification

Electricity demand is growing again after decades of flat load. Every new large load competes for the same grid capacity, and arranging a connection takes time.

AI and data centers

The pursuit of superintelligence is driving a new class of large, always-on electricity loads. These projects need sites with power certainty on timelines the grid was not originally planned for.

Storage and onsite generation

Batteries and behind-the-meter generation are becoming part of how large loads get connected, not just how the grid stays balanced. Projects that plan for them early keep more options open.
